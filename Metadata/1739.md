### DEFINICIÓN - ESPAÑOL
La superficie regada se refiere a la tierra equipada con infraestructura y equipamiento de riego para suministrar agua a los cultivos.

El equipamiento debe estar en condiciones de funcionamiento, aunque no necesariamente en uso durante el año de referencia. Comprende las áreas equipadas para riego con control total mediante riego superficial, por aspersión o localizado, así como las superficies regadas por derivación de crecidas (derivación de aguas de inundación para regar cultivos) y las zonas bajas equipadas, incluidos humedales, fondos de valles interiores y áreas de recesión de inundaciones. Excluye el riego manual de plantas mediante cubos, regaderas u otros dispositivos.

### DEFINICIÓN - INGLÉS
Irrigated area refers to land equipped with irrigation infrastructure and equipment to provide water to crops.

The equipment must be in working order but need not be in use in the reference year. It covers areas equipped for full control irrigation using surface, sprinkler or localized irrigation, as well as spate irrigation areas (diversion of flood water to irrigate crops) and equipped lowlands, including wetlands, inland valley bottoms and flood recession areas. It excludes manual watering of plants using buckets, watering cans or other devices.

### METODOLOGÍA - INGLÉS
Data are sourced from the FAOSTAT database, which compiles national figures from agricultural censuses, sample surveys and official studies reported by national statistical offices and ministries of agriculture. Data are compiled annually, but because the underlying censuses and surveys may be conducted less frequently, some country-year values are interpolated or carried forward.

The irrigated area figure corresponds to the FAOSTAT variable "Land area equipped for irrigation," which encompasses: areas under full or partial control irrigation, where water supply to crops is fully or partially managed; spate irrigation areas, where seasonal floodwaters are diverted onto agricultural land; and equipped lowland areas, where water control infrastructure is in place to manage flood recession or waterlogging conditions.

Land use indicators describe the socioeconomic use of land, that is, the activities for which land is used as a natural or economic resource. This is distinguished from land cover, which instead describes the biophysical coverage of land (for example, by crops, grass, broad-leaved forest, or built-up area). These concepts and definitions are consistent with the System of Environmental-Economic Accounting (SEEA) Central Framework, the international statistical standard for measuring environmental contributions to the economy and economic impacts on the environment.
